CHIKKAMAGALURU CHRONICLES

History of the Coffee Cradle

Chikkamagaluru is famed as the cradle of coffee in India, where the saint Baba Budan first planted seven coffee beans smuggled from Yemen in 1670. Beyond its plantations, the region contains deep roots tied to the early foundations of the Hoysala Empire and holds spectacular high-elevation stone mountain shrines, architectural marvels, and deep monastic lineages.

01 / 10

Vidyashankara Temple (Sringeri)

A spectacular 14th-century temple blending Hoysala and Vijayanagara architectural designs, engineered with 12 foundational zodiac pillars designed with astronomical precision.

Built in 1338 AD to commemorate Vidyatirtha, the revered guru of the founders of the Vijayanagara Empire (Harihara and Bukka), this monument is a triumph of mathematical stonecraft. The structure stands entirely on an elevated golden-ratio plinth overlooking the clear waters of the Tunga River.

The most defining feature is the assembly hall's twelve pillars, each representing a sign of the zodiac (Rasis). The builders calculated their positions so precisely that the morning rays of the sun hit the exact pillar corresponding to the current solar month. The temple's curved apsidal layout borrows heavily from early Buddhist chaitya patterns, showcasing a transition from soapstone carvings to granite monuments.

02 / 10

Amrutesvara Temple (Amrutapura)

An outstanding soapstone monument built in 1196 AD under Hoysala supervision, featuring an open hall wrapped completely in fine narratively carved panels.

Commissioned by Amrutheshwara Dandanayaka, a prominent commander under the Hoysala King Veera Ballala II, this temple is a brilliant display of chloritic schist (soapstone) architecture. It follows a star-shaped layout, which increases the external wall surface area to allow for continuous bands of micro-sculptures.

The exterior walls feature 140 meticulously detailed panels illustrating episodes from the Indian epics. The sequence includes the birth and exploits of Lord Krishna along the northern wall, followed by a detailed progression of the Mahabharata and Ramayana stories, carved with such delicate precision that even the reins of horses and expressions of soldiers remain perfectly legible centuries later.

03 / 10

Baba Budangiri Shrine

An ancient, revered cave shrine nestled high in the Western Ghats, celebrated as a historic pilgrimage spot honoring both Sufi saint Baba Budan and Guru Dattatreya.

Historically known as Chandra Drona Parvatha due to its natural crescent shape, this high-altitude range is deeply significant to Indian agricultural and spiritual history. In 1670 AD, the Sufi saint Baba Budan returned from a pilgrimage to Mecca, bringing seven fertile coffee seeds hidden in his robes. He planted them on these slopes, establishing India's very first coffee plantation.

The central destination is a dark, underground cave shrine. It is uniquely shared by both Hindu and Muslim communities, who revere it as the residence of Guru Dattatreya and Hazrat Dada Hayat Mir Qalandar, making it a prominent symbol of regional syncretic heritage.

04 / 10

Veera Narayana Temple (Belavadi)

A massive trikuta (three-shrine) Hoysala complex, showcasing a grand assembly hall fitted with over 108 polished, lathe-turned stone columns.

Constructed in the early 13th century by King Veera Ballala II, Belavadi is mentioned in the epic Mahabharata as Ekachakra Nagara, the legendary site where the Pandava prince Bhima defeated the demon Bakasura. Architecturally, it stands out for its exceptional scale and balanced layout.

The long, covered complex connects three separate shrines dedicated to Veera Narayana, Venugopala, and Yoganarasimha. The central sabhamandapa (gathering hall) is supported by bell-shaped, mirror-polished stone columns that look metallic. Each pillar was turned on primitive lathe systems to achieve a smooth finish that reflects the light filtering through the stone screens.

05 / 10

Sringeri Sharada Peetham

The first monastic center established by Adi Shankaracharya in the 8th century, set along the banks of the Tunga River as a prominent seat of Advaita Vedanta philosophy.

Legend states that when Adi Shankaracharya walked along the Tunga River, he witnessed a remarkable sight: a cobra holding its hood open like an umbrella to shield a spawning frog from the blistering heat. Moved by this display of natural harmony, he chose this peaceful spot to establish his first southern monastery.

The complex contains the ancient Sharadamba Temple, which originally housed a sandalwood idol of the deity that was later replaced with a gold statue during the Vijayanagara period. The center has preserved ancient palm-leaf manuscripts and traditional Vedic teaching methodologies for over twelve centuries without interruption.

06 / 10

Kodanda Rama Temple (Hiremagalur)

An ancient shrine featuring a unique arrangement of historical idols, displaying early Dravidian stonework with Hoysala and Chola modifications.

According to regional records, this site was where the warrior Parasurama requested to see Lord Rama's wedding ceremony. Consequently, the temple's central sanctum displays a distinct iconographic layout: Sita is placed on Rama's right side instead of the traditional left, and Lakshmana stands to his left holding his bow.

The structural core dates back to the Chola and Hoysala periods, while the grand open assembly porch was added during subsequent Vijayanagara renovations. The temple keeps local epigraphical stone slabs that detail land grants awarded by medieval rulers to support the upkeep of this sanctuary.

07 / 10

Kallathigiri Cave Temple

An ancient cave temple built into a natural rock face directly behind a cascading waterfall, featuring stone carvings from the Vijayanagara era.

Also known as Kalhatti Falls, this destination seamlessly integrates natural water landscapes with early religious architecture. Local tradition links the site to the Vedic sage Agastya, who is said to have spent long periods in meditation within these rocky clefts.

The temple structures are built into the cliff side, where the waterfall drops 122 meters across the rocks. Worshippers pass through the natural spray to reach a small stone shrine featuring three guardian elephant carvings attributed to late Vijayanagara stonecraft.

08 / 10

Balehonnur Shrimath

One of the oldest religious seats of the Veerashaiva community, situated on the banks of the Bhadra River and holding historical gold-plated artifacts.

The Sri Rambhapuri Peetha at Balehonnur is historically recognized as one of the five foundational monastic centers (Pancha Peethas) of Veerashaivism in India. Its origins trace back to the early medieval period, established by the saint Renukacharya.

The monastery contains an ornate temple dedicated to Veerabhadra, featuring rows of detailed stone carvings. It houses a collection of copperplate inscriptions, palm-leaf manuscripts, and gold-plated processional ornaments gifted by regional Hoysala, Keladi, and Mysore rulers over centuries of continuous institutional influence.

Horanadu Annapoorneshwari

An ancient shrine set deep in the hills, housing a prominent standing golden statue of Goddess Annapoorneshwari.

Formally named Sri Adishaktyatmaka Annapoorneshwari Ammanavara Temple, this site has records of continuous worship dating back to the late 8th century, established by sage Agastya. The temple is located in a high valley surrounded by dense forests and hills.

The main sanctuary houses a golden image of the deity holding a ritual ladle and vessel, representing eternal nourishment. Following a long-standing tradition of hospitality, the temple community provides complimentary multi-course vegetarian meals and accommodation to every visitor who makes the journey across the mountain passes.

10 / 10

Angadi Hoysala Temple Site

The historical birthplace of the Hoysala Dynasty, containing early stone ruins and the temple site where the legendary founder Sala struck down a mythical tiger.

Historically known as Sasakapura, this quiet valley is where the Hoysala empire began. According to ancient inscriptions, the young warrior Sala was studying here under a Jain guru when a wild tiger attacked their temple sanctuary. The guru called out "Poy Sala!" (meaning "Strike, Sala!"). Sala killed the predator, and the phrase became the name of the new dynasty.

The dynamic encounter became the royal stone emblem seen on every subsequent Hoysala monument. Today, the secluded site contains the preserved ruins of several 10th-century temples dedicated to Vasantha Parameshwari, Chennakesava, and Mallikarjuna, showcasing the earliest experiments in soft soapstone carving.
